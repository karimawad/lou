Important notices

Effective October 5, 2026

Lou is not a tax preparer

Lou is software. It does not give tax advice and it does not review, sign or file your return. You sign your own return, and you are responsible for it. Check every number against your documents before you file. Every result Lou produces carries a reminder to do this.

When to get a professional

Lou flags situations that need judgment, and it handles many common ones. Consider a qualified cross-border tax professional if you have a complex business or rental property, large or complex investments, foreign trusts or corporations, an estate, a recent move between countries, or any situation where Lou tells you it is unsure. The review package in Lou is designed to hand to a professional.

Quebec

Lou does not do Quebec returns yet. Quebec has its own return, its own slips and its own rules that affect the US foreign tax credit, and Lou has not been checked against them. If you lived in Quebec on December 31 of the tax year, Lou stops and tells you so rather than guessing. Lou works for the other provinces and territories.

Catching up on missed years

Lou can prepare the paperwork for the IRS Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures: delinquent returns for the most recent three years, FBAR worksheets for the most recent six years, a worksheet for Form 14653, and a cover sheet and checklist for mailing. The procedure is only for people whose failure to file was not willful, who lived outside the US, and who are not under an IRS examination or investigation. Lou asks screening questions and stops, or tells you to see a professional, when your answers point that way. Lou does not write your Form 14653 statement, which you sign under penalty of perjury, and it does not prepare amended returns (Form 1040-X) or the domestic procedure for people who were in the US most of the year. Lou's interest figure is an estimate and the IRS may bill or refund a difference. The IRS can still examine a streamlined submission, and the penalty relief is lost if the failure turns out to be willful. If you knew about the rules, were contacted by the IRS, kept a home in the US, or have large account balances, talk to a qualified tax professional before you use it.

How Lou stays accurate

Tax amounts, tables and form field mappings come from official IRS forms and instructions and CRA documents. Tax rates and limits are specific to each tax year, and Lou supports only the years it lists. Rules change, and some points have no clear official answer. Where that is the case Lou shows a note instead of picking quietly. Reading a photo or scan is never perfect, so Lou marks uncertain values "Please check" for you to confirm.

Filing

Lou does not send anything to the IRS, the CRA or FinCEN, and is not an IRS authorized e-file provider. You mail or e-file the forms yourself. The FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) can only be filed by you through FinCEN BSA E-Filing. Lou gives you a worksheet to copy from.
